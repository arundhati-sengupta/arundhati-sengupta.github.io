About

I'm a computational linguist with more than ten years of experience in linguistic data analysis and language technology, across both speech and text. My foundation is language. I hold a Ph.D. in Linguistics from the Graduate Center, City University of New York, where I also taught NLP-focused courses. My dissertation studied Bengali grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) conversion and included the first systematic evaluation of large language models on Bengali G2P.

Most of my work covers the whole data lifecycle: designing collection protocols, writing annotation guidelines, training annotators and checking their work, and then measuring data and model quality with statistically careful evaluation. I work closely with linguists, annotators and engineers, and I'm often the one translating between them.

Earlier, I spent seven years at the Central Institute of Indian Languages leading multilingual speech and text corpus projects. I then worked at Google, evaluating speech data and acoustic and phonemic models, and did linguistic QA for Lionbridge.

I'm a native speaker of English and Bengali, work professionally in Hindi, and have analyzed data in many high- and low-resource South Asian languages and scripts.
